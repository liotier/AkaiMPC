**Chord Progression Generator: November 2026 update**

The Chord Progression Generator, the free browser tool that builds chord progressions and exports them as `.progression` files for MPC Pad Perform, as MIDI files, or as chord sheets for keys, guitar and staff, has a large update. Most of it serves one aim: every chord on every pad musically right, and saying plainly what it is.

Try it: https://liotier.github.io/AkaiMPC/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/

**More to play with**

- 184 progressions in 22 genres, up from 173. Film/Cinematic grows from 7 to 18, with the devices film and game scores lean on: chromatic mediants, the hexatonic pole, Lydian and Dorian loops, the Hero Cadence, and the ascending line cliché, the spy-theme Em, Em#5, Em6.
- A sixth voicing style, Pedal: the first chord's bass note is held under every chord that contains it, so the harmony moves over a note that stays.
- Film/Cinematic progressions, and four others, fill their top row with film-score moves, chromatic mediants and mode flips, instead of classical secondary dominants.

**Chords that are right**

- Minor-key progressions get minor-key palettes. They used to be offered the major key's chords: in A minor, Bm and F#m, labelled as if they belonged.
- Twelve minor-key progressions that played the wrong chords are fixed. "Hotel California" opened on Ab and Db where the Eagles play G and C.
- No pad repeats another: a grid never spends two pads on the same notes under two names, in either mode.
- Stacked-fourths chords are named for what they are, C7sus4(no5) and Cm7(11), not Csus4.
- A progression listed under several genres keeps the genre you picked it from: its palette, its export name, and after a reload.

**Names you can read**

- Every chord is spelled in its key: F#m in A major, never Gbm, and the line cliché's raised fifth is E G B#. The note names under each pad and the staff follow.
- Hover a pad and it says what the chord is, then what it does: "Major triad on the flattened seventh degree", then "Borrowed from Mixolydian, gives rock/blues flavor...". In all six languages.
- The summary line under each variant (Modal Interchange, Secondary Dominants, Chromatic...) follows one rule now and tells the truth: I-iv-I-V is no longer called a "Diatonic progression".

**Scale Mode, rebuilt**

- Sixteen pads, sixteen different chords, in all 34 scales; Whole Tone used to offer eight. The symmetric scales show the chords they are known for: 7♭5 and 9♯5 chords in Whole Tone, and four dominant sevenths a minor third apart in the half-whole diminished scale.
- Roman numerals follow the same convention as the palettes: Dorian's Eb is ♭III, Pentatonic Major's G is V.
- Every description is true for the scale it describes.

**The packs**

The pre-built downloads under the generator grow to 1 094 `.progression` files (key of C; Pad Perform transposes on import), up from 839, and 13 135 MIDI files in all twelve keys, up from 10 065.

Heads-up: some files change name, namely the twelve corrected minor-key progressions, those with stacked-fourths chords, and a few minor-key variants. Files you already saved are untouched; download them again to get the corrected chords.

**Under the hood**

Every pad the generator can produce, 218 496 of them across every progression, key, style and scale, is checked on every change: the right notes, the right spelling, no duplicate sound, a true numeral and a true description. The full list of changes is in the changelog: https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md

Feedback is welcome, above all from MPC owners: if a file misbehaves in Pad Perform, or a chord sounds wrong to you, say so here or open an issue at https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/issues

Free and open source (Unlicense). It runs offline once loaded, and nothing leaves your browser.
